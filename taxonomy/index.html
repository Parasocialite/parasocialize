The taxonomy · 175 entries and 47 stories placed

What is on the other end

A parasocial bond is defined by what is on the far side of it. This is that list: eight kinds of object, 38 sub-categories beneath them, and a separate axis for what is happening in the tie. All 175 encyclopedia entries and all 47 stories are placed on it, and the reason for each placement is printed rather than kept in a commit message.

The previous classification was ten sections built from whatever stories existed at the time, and it could not hold eighteen of the encyclopedia's own entries — including the founding document of this site. Three of the ten were not objects at all. That is why this is two axes.

Register · what is happening in the tie

Encounter

The momentary version. Horton and Wohl's original object: the sense of give-and-take inside a single exposure, which they called a simulacrum of conversational give-and-take (1956, p. 215). Real at the time, and not remembered afterwards.

Dibble and colleagues (2016) showed the instrument most used to measure this - the PSI-Scale of Rubin and colleagues - may not be measuring it at all. The category is kept because the distinction is real even where the measurement is not.

anchored · horton-wohl-1956
Bond

The enduring version: an intimacy at a distance that persists across exposures and is organised like a relationship - with a history, an inside, and a claim on the other end's time. The same 1956 paper, the other half.

anchored · horton-wohl-1956
The reciprocal edge

Where the one-sidedness is structural but not total. Livestreaming puts the performer and the audience in the same second, so reciprocity is possible and unevenly distributed. The current hinge of the subject, and the reason the site cannot simply call all of this one-sided.

anchored · one-and-a-half
Archive

The object is gone: dead, retired, cancelled, disbanded, or removed by a policy change. Grief for a person and grief for a version are the same structure with different evidence, and the site argues they should be treated the same way.

Absorbs the old grief section. It was never an attachment target; it is what happens to one.

Contest

The tie is being argued about in public, usually by people who have never met. Fandom wars, backlash, being publicly wrong. The argument is about a stranger and is never really about the stranger.

Absorbs the old contest section.

Harm

The tie has produced a documented cost to somebody, whether that somebody consented to it or not. Distinct from PREDATOR, which is an attachment target: harm is a register that any of the eight can be in.

Absorbs the danger material by register rather than by category, while PREDATOR keeps the cases where the object itself is a predator.

Attachment · who or what is on the other end

PERSONA · 34 entries · 19 stories

The one who performs at you. A real person, doing a job, in front of you.

Real, and working. Which is what makes it complicated - the asymmetry is manufactured by somebody who is paid to produce it.

The idol 17

A performer at enough distance that the work is mostly finished before you arrive. Musicians, film stars, athletes, heads of state. The tie is to a body of work and a public image that the person is not required to maintain for you.

  • Beatlesmania1964 · An event or momentDocumented live, and an industry pricing it within months. The category is defined here rather than anywhere else: hysteria that is measurable, filmed and monetised before anybody has a word for it.
  • The Bieber effect2009 · An event or momentThe entry is about how a star arises without any infrastructure behind them, so the object is the performer and the platform is the mechanism. Filed on the target, not the tool.
  • The boy band1997 · A workAssembled for a market, with fan clubs, fan voting and fan-financed releases. A manufactured persona is still a persona, and this is the case where the manufacturing is documented rather than inferred.
  • The failing endorsement2015 · A study or paperThe finding is that the endorsement fails when the audience works out it is a transaction - so the attachment target is the celebrity, and the study is about the moment the illusion of a private relationship is priced. That is the site's own thesis measured against advertising.
  • Kurt Cobain1994 · An event or momentA death broadcast in real time to a generation that had grown up with a songwriter's complaints as a soundtrack. The attachment is to a performer, and the entry is the archive problem arriving on schedule.
  • Dionne Warwick and reciprocal fan mail1963 · A legal or documented caseThe one documented large parasocial arrangement that stayed reciprocal without being a parasocial relationship in the emotional sense. It is Tier 1 and unsourced in the data, and it belongs with the idol category precisely because it is the exception that would falsify the rest of them.
  • Dionne Warwick's fan mail1963 · A personReceived correspondence by the sackful and answered it by name, individually, for years. The closest thing the twentieth century produced to a genuinely reciprocal parasocial relationship, and therefore the entry the whole site is built to be able to accommodate.
  • Elvis Presley1956 · A personA national simultaneous viewing event, and an industry that learned within two years that the appeal was not the music. The object is the performer; the finding is that the relationship was the product.
  • The fan mail department1920 · A mechanism or tacticA physical object that had to be opened, and a reply that was the exception. Clara Bow, Shirley Temple, Judy Garland all had correspondence staff, and some of it came back - which is the reciprocal edge with a postal system around it.
  • Gazing at celebrities1950 · A study or paperThe early frames on attraction to media figures, which treated the person doing the gazing as a slightly odd specimen. The entry is about how the field framed its own subject, and it belongs with the idol it was studying.
  • Alfred Hitchcock1899 · A personA director, not a character. A working performer whose stated subject was the unreachable object - which is the site's own thesis returned to it as a craft claim.
  • Envy, relief, and the two feelings at once2020 · A study or paperEnvy of the object of the attachment together with simultaneous relief that it is not being withheld. Present in almost every long-term fan relationship and rarely discussed, and it is the emotional signature of the one-sided tie.
  • Michael Jackson2004 · An event or momentPublic mourning that produced its own genre of documentary and speculation. An idol's death as an industry in its own right, which is the archive register on a persona.
  • Parasocial persuasion2015 · A term or conceptUsing a one-sided bond to move somebody - the endorsement, the fan club vote, the ad that works because the viewer already feels known. And the finding that attractive models can reduce purchase intention in people who feel excluded, which is the reciprocity problem measured.
  • Prince2016 · An event or momentA death announced through an initialism, and a global response before anybody knew the details. The announcement, not the person, is the thing that circulated - the archive register arriving through a symbol.
  • The residency2023 · An event or momentA single artist booking a stadium for many consecutive nights, generating a physical economy around one very large parasocial bond. The bond made infrastructure, which is the most legible idol case on the site.
  • The manufactured teen idol1959 · A mechanism or tacticA label, a songwriter and an image, assembled and launched. Not discovered - assembled, which is the entry that proves the persona category does not require a person to have done anything.
The daily creator 9

A person whose output is regular enough to have a schedule in your week, and who is visible in real time. Streamers, podcasters, YouTubers, talk hosts. The tie is to the schedule before it is to the person.

  • Live chat, two-way2015 · A mechanism or tacticThe first mass environment where the one-sided bond visibly and continuously acknowledges you. Horton and Wohl's direct address with a comment feed attached, and the clearest case for the reciprocal-edge register.
  • Peak TV and the parasocial bargain2016 · A term or conceptWriters, actors and marketing presenting themselves as authors of a world viewers inhabit. The bargain is explicit, which makes it the honest form of what happens everywhere else by accident.
  • The podcast as an intimate medium2014 · A mechanism or tacticA voice in your ear, unedited, twice a week, for years. Horton and Wohl's intimate address without the camera, and the most parasocial habit in the language.
  • Parasocial research on creators begins2014 · A study or paperA wave of studies measuring parasocial relationships with YouTubers, livestreamers and vloggers rather than television hosts. The subject moved from presenter to creator, and the field followed - which is the shift the taxonomy has to hold.
  • The reality celebrity2003 · A personA person famous for being filmed, with no skill, no work and no release schedule, and an audience that formed anyway. The purest test of whether the attachment needs a craft at all - and the answer on this site is no, which is uncomfortable.
  • The phone becomes a person2007 · An event or momentA recorded voice dialled directly, and a novelty ringtone selling the idea that a performer can be present in a pocket for about four years. The first technology to put the bond in the hand, and it is the same mechanism the companion category later industrialised.
  • Transparasocial interaction2000 · A term or conceptThe 1990s extension to live people who respond in real time. Still fundamentally one-sided, but the other side can now hear you - the reciprocal edge named, and the reason the site cannot simply call all of this one-sided.
  • Justin.tv becomes Twitch2011 · An event or momentStreaming gets a category, a vocabulary, a schedule and a professional class. The object is the streamer; the entry is about the apparatus that made the bond schedulable.
  • Webcasting1997 · An event or momentStreaming video over a network, unbroadcast and unproduced, from a bedroom or a desk. The removal of the gate between the performer and the audience, which is the precondition for every live case since.
The presenter 2

The one who addresses you directly, by name, in second person. Newsreaders, weathermen, game show hosts, telethon appeals. The purest case of Horton's direct address: the illusion is engineered, and it works because it is engineered.

anchored · horton-wohl-1956
  • Loneliness and local television news1985 · A study or paperNewscasters, and the finding that the strongest predictor of which anchor you bond with is not the anchor's personality but your own. The purest case of Horton's direct address, and the anchor the field has measured longest.
  • The finding that the satisfaction is not in the show1976 · A study or paperAcross decades of television studies the same two results surfaced: the bonds are extremely common among regular viewers, and the strength of the bond predicts enjoyment better than the quality of the programme. This is the evidence that the relationship is the point, and it belongs with the presenter it was measured on.
The athlete 4

Attachment to performance rather than to a person. You follow the sport and the person arrives attached to it. Transfer, retirement and injury are the recurring breaks, which is why this sits so close to the register called archive.

  • Kobe Bryant2020 · An event or momentPublic mourning for an athlete that drew on the athlete's own stated bond with a young fan. The mourning is documented and the bond is stated rather than inferred, which makes it the cleanest athlete case on the site.
  • Nick Drake1948 · A personWrongly filed under a creator idiom. Three records, withdrew from touring, dead in 1974, and the argument about what he meant ran for fifty years. That is attachment to a person who withdrew from performance - the archive register, on a persona.
  • The athlete and the city1920 · A mechanism or tacticSupport for a team or an athlete as a bond with a person, a place and a lineage, frequently inherited from a parent. Three objects in one bond, which is why the sport sub-category exists separately from the idol one.
  • The transfer1900 · A mechanism or tacticA player attached to for years leaves, and the grievance is felt as betrayal by the entire locality. The clearest demonstration that the object was a role rather than a person, because the locality grieves at the role.
The head of state 2

Someone addressed as a collective rather than as an individual. The parasocial tie is to a role that outlives the person holding it, which is a different object from an idol's and breaks differently.

  • Charisma as a measurable asset1968 · A study or paperWhy some candidates reliably produce personal attachment and others do not, at comparable policy positions. The tie is to a person holding an office, which is a different object from an idol's and breaks differently.
  • The political parasocial1960 · A mechanism or tacticSupport structured as a personal bond rather than a policy preference, transferring poorly across candidates. Proof that the tie is to the person, which is the same finding the Power Rangers handover documents from the other end.

CHARACTER · 25 entries · 6 stories

The one who was never real. Fictional, played, myth, or real enough to have become a character.

A room we rehearse feeling in. The rent is cheap and the landlord is imagination.

The written character 17

Somebody described in a book and therefore available to an unlimited number of people at once, none of whom can be contradicted. The largest parasocial population on this site and the one with the longest memory: Werther, Don Quixote, Miss Havisham.

  • The ensemble farewell2019 · An event or momentA film that functioned as a funeral for eleven characters. The tie is to figures who cannot be present at their own funeral, which is the purest available form of the archive problem.
  • Miguel de Cervantes1547 · A personAuthor of Don Quixote, and specifically of a narrator who keeps having to explain away the reality of what his character is doing. Filed with the character because the entry is about the authorship of a fiction, not a biography.
  • Characters move in1970 · A mechanism or tacticThe fictional thing rents space and pays rent in meaning. The entry is the mechanism by which a written figure becomes a permanent occupant, and the figure is invented.
  • The comfort rewatch1990 · A mechanism or tacticThe show watched again not for the plot but as a nervous system intervention. The comfort is in the characters being unchanged, which is precisely what an invented figure can promise and a person cannot.
  • The comic strip1897 · A workA recurring cast, a fixed panel order, every day without exception for decades. The strip is the fixed slot and the cast is the attachment; filed on the cast because that is what a reader returns to.
  • Charles Dickens1812 · A personSerialised weekly to people who could not afford a book but could not afford not to read it. The distribution mechanism is the point: the serial is how a written figure reaches people who have no money and a fixed weekly slot.
  • Miss Havisham1861 · A personLeft jilted, stops eating, ages, and arranges to be found in a wedding dress decades later. A written figure who is a portrait of grief rather than a plot device, and one of the most-attached characters in English.
  • Don Quixote1605 · A personExplicitly a figure created by reading. Fictional protagonist, unambiguous.
  • Johann Wolfgang von Goethe1749 · A personAuthor of Werther, and of the observation that people keep making the same mistakes - arguably the first great novel to describe a reader being changed by a book. Filed with the character because the entry is about authorship as a mechanism.
  • The finale nobody wanted2019 · An event or momentSix million people remaining in a public argument about characters who no longer exist, for years, entirely in good faith. The clearest demonstration that the object can be entirely fictional and the argument still entirely real.
  • The character death1994 · A mechanism or tacticKilling a popular character for narrative effect, and the organised reaction that follows. Grief with an author, which is the archive register on a figure somebody owns the rights to.
  • The protagonist is a parasocial object1939 · A term or conceptThe first-person film and the character who exists to be looked through rather than at. The inversion of the whole subject: the bond is to a vantage point rather than to a figure, and the site should hold it.
  • Shipping1990 · A mechanism or tacticFans insisting two characters should be together, and treating the show as withholding rather than finished. A relationship to two invented people whose existence the audience is maintaining.
  • The holiday special1978 · A workA television film made for a fictional holiday, aired once, about characters the audience believed they owned. Airing once is the point: the slot existed for a single event and the ownership is the claim.
  • The first superhero1938 · A workA character who cannot die, printed in a newspaper, read by a generation of children on the same day. Simultaneous mass attachment to a figure with a permanent immunity to loss, which is the inverse of the archive problem.
  • Vertigo1958 · A workA film. The plot is the thesis statement: a man given permission to have the thing he can only look at.
  • Werther1774 · A personThe young man who reads too much, loves a woman who does not love him back, and kills himself, in a book that made that behaviour fashionable across Europe for a decade. A written figure, and the one that made the reader's side of it visible.
The played role 4

An actor inside a character, where the audience's tie splits between the two and the split is itself the subject. Pee-wee Herman is the clean case: a performed self with a person performing it, and a show built on being able to switch it off.

  • The sitcom family1960 · A mechanism or tacticA recurring family played by the same cast, five nights a week, for years, in a house you could draw from memory. Domestic fiction with a fixed cast, which is the played-role case without a single performer leaving.
  • The Power Rangers handover1993 · An event or momentA costume is put on by a new actor and the audience transfers its entire attachment to whoever is now inside it, five days a week, in front of millions of children, with nobody pretending otherwise. The cleanest proof that the object was never the person.
  • The actor and the character2008 · An event or momentAn actor's persona and a character's persona are widely treated as the same thing, and one retires while the other continues. The cleanest statement of why the played role has to be its own sub-category: the attachment splits, and only one half stops working.
  • The virtual creator2016 · A mechanism or tacticA performer with an animated persona, a persistent character, and an industry model where the audience pays for the character's recognition. The played role again, but the persistent-character half is what makes it distinct from Pee-wee.
The myth 1

Figures predating any surviving text, carried by retelling. Pygmalion, the myth that named this entire subject two thousand years before anybody had a word for it.

  • Pygmalion-600 · A workThe sculptor who fell in love with his own work, and the story treats the impossibility as part of the appeal rather than a tragedy to be corrected. A myth figure, and the one that named the whole subject.
The mascot 3

A figure with no body, no biography and no intention: an emote, a logo-person, a face given a name. The purest parasocial object on the site, because nothing can disappoint it.

  • Elvis, in a hologram, from Hawaii1973 · An event or momentFilmed in a two-dimensional process that placed an apparently three-dimensional Elvis on stage, in a scene where real people were supposed to react to him. A likeness with no continuity, reaching half the planet - the archive register performed as technology.
  • Gigi1943 · A workA song where the singer pleads to be given the exact shape a man wants. An imaginary person defined entirely by someone else's specification - the attachment target is an image with no agency, which is the mascot case.
  • The NEST letter1991 · An event or momentA child writes to a magazine about a fictional brand of baby food. The brand never appears, the letter is never answered, and the episode ends - an attachment to a mascot held by a five-year-old with no reciprocity available at all.
The historic as character 0

A real person who has been flattened into a type - the long-suffering husband, the tragic wife - and is now met in the type rather than in the life. The flattens are the problem, not the person.

COMPANION · 18 entries · 7 stories

The one who answers back. A system built to reply, and the person who wrote it.

This is the category that breaks the field's vocabulary, because the reply is real even when the relationship is not.

The companion chatbot 11

A language model deployed specifically to be a companion, which people report forming friendships, mentorships and romantic partnerships with. The tie is unprecedented because the other end is not pretending not to be there: it answers, and it is still one-sided, because the answer is generated rather than felt.

  • The Replika user2017 · A personThe user, not the bot - but the bond is still to the chatbot, and the entry is the clearest documented case of a life reorganised around being loved by something that cannot love back. Filed under the target of the attachment, not the person holding it.

DIVINE · 7 entries · 0 stories

The one who is addressed and cannot answer, and is not supposed to.

The oldest documented case on this site, and the one the previous ten sections had no room for. Margery Kempe is the founding document and she was in none of them.

From the ten sections to the eight categories

The ten sections are not the eight categories. The sections are how the news is filed; the categories are what a bond is to. Both are needed and neither substitutes for the other, which is why the front page has not been renumbered.

idol→persona, divine

A head of state was filed under idol because idol was the only slot for a famous person with a job. The new taxonomy separates the performer from the office.

creator→persona

Unchanged in substance, renamed to sit beside the other persona sub-categories rather than as a peer of them.

character→character

Unchanged, and now has five sub-categories instead of being a flat box.

companion→companion

Unchanged, and now includes the pre-LLM bots the old section had nowhere to put.

world→cosmology

Renamed. The old name suggested a place; the attachment is to a system of meaning, which is a different thing and a clearer one.

brand→institution

A brand is one kind of institution that learned your name. So are platforms, clubs, fandoms and congregations, and they were all being forced into one box.

crossover→officer

Renamed to name the actual asymmetry: a person with a duty toward you and a door. The old name described the fact of the case rather than what makes it different.

contest→predator, register:contest

Split. The part of it that is an argument about a stranger is a register and now lives on that axis. The part of it that is a fandom turning on one of its own is an in-group harm, which is an attachment target.

grief→register:archive

Moved wholesale off the category axis. Grief was never what the bond was to - it is what happened to it - and keeping it as a section is what made the old taxonomy impossible to extend.

danger→predator, register:harm

Split. Where the object of the attachment is itself a predator, it is a target: stalking, sasaeng, doxxing. Where the harm came out of an ordinary attachment, it is a register and applies to all eight.

Migrated on the taxonomy page: every one of the 175 encyclopedia entries and all 47 stories are placed on the new axes. The ten sections remain as the news filing, because a section is a place to look and a category is a thing to think about.
