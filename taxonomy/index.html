The taxonomy · 177 entries and 47 stories placed

What is on the other end

A parasocial bond is defined by what is on the far side of it. This is that list: eight kinds of object, 38 sub-categories beneath them, and 6 registers for what is happening in the tie. All 177 encyclopedia entries and all 47 stories are placed on it, and the reason for each placement is printed rather than kept in a commit message.

The previous classification was ten sections built from whatever stories existed at the time, and it could not hold eighteen of the encyclopedia's own entries — including the founding document of this site. Three of the ten were not objects at all. That is why this is two axes.

Register · what is happening in the tie · 6

Encounter

The momentary version. Horton and Wohl's original object: the sense of give-and-take inside a single exposure, which they called a simulacrum of conversational give-and-take (1956, p. 215). Real at the time, and not remembered afterwards.

Test

Does anything survive the exposure? If the feeling is still there tomorrow, in the absence of another contact, this is a bond and not an encounter. The test is deliberately about persistence rather than intensity, because the two feel identical from the inside while the literature has treated them as different objects since 1956.

Not

Not parasocial interaction in the loose sense, which is the word most people reach for and which covers all six of these. Not the single viewing either - Horton and Wohl's simulacrum of give-and-take can happen during a soap opera you have watched for twenty years, so the boundary is not the length of the exposure but what is left afterwards.

Mechanism

Recognition inside one exposure. The other end acknowledges the feeling without acknowledging the person, which is exactly what makes it feel real: something is received, and the reception is not imaginary, only the recipient's presence in it is. Horton and Wohl's phrase is a simulacrum, not a fiction, and the distinction is load-bearing.

Dibble and colleagues (2016) showed the instrument most used to measure this - the PSI-Scale of Rubin and colleagues - may not be measuring it at all. The category is kept because the distinction is real even where the measurement is not.

Bond

The enduring version: an intimacy at a distance that persists across exposures and is organised like a relationship - with a history, an inside, and a claim on the other end's time. The same 1956 paper, the other half.

Test

Is there a history? A bond that cannot be asked what came before it is an encounter with a good memory attached. The three things that make it a bond rather than a repeated encounter are a shared past, a sense of an inside, and a claim on the other end's time - somebody who is late is noticed.

Not

Not attachment to an institution. The loyalty-scheme case is filed under BRAND because the tie transfers to a successor; a bond does not, which is why a club can be inherited and a relationship cannot. Not a habit either - a ritual with no history in it is the scheduling case on the attachment axis.

Mechanism

Accumulation with a narrative. The other end is treated as continuous across absences, so the tie acquires a plot: things happened, and they happened between us. That is what lets grief arrive when the object dies, and it is why the archive register exists as a separate thing rather than as an unpleasant ending to a bond.

The reciprocal edge

Where the one-sidedness is structural but not total. Livestreaming puts the performer and the audience in the same second, so reciprocity is possible and unevenly distributed. The current hinge of the subject, and the reason the site cannot simply call all of this one-sided.

Test

Could the other end have answered? If the channel permits a reply that would actually be seen by the person you sent it to, this is the reciprocal edge. The test is about the channel, not the outcome - an unanswered message and a message never delivered are different, and only the first is an edge rather than a dead end.

Not

Not a bond that happens to feel mutual. The distinction from BOND is structural: the edge is a property of the medium, not of the feeling, which is why a livestream can be one-sided for a particular viewer while the format is structurally reciprocal. Not a fandom body either, where the reciprocity runs between fans rather than toward the object.

Mechanism

Asymmetry made structural and visible. The reply is possible, so the one-sidedness is no longer a property of the world but a fact about who happened to be in the room - which is a harder thing to accept than an impossibility, and the current hinge of the whole subject. This is why COMPANION and PERSONA cannot be treated as one category: the same attachment target behaves differently depending on which register it sits in.

Archive

The object is gone: dead, retired, cancelled, disbanded, or removed by a policy change. Grief for a person and grief for a version are the same structure with different evidence, and the site argues they should be treated the same way.

Test

Is the object gone? Dead, retired, cancelled, disbanded, removed by policy, or simply unreachable because the platform changed. If the person or thing is unavailable in a way that will not be reversed, this is the archive, and grief is what it feels like from the inside.

Not

Not harm. The archive is about loss and harm is about cost, and an entry can be in both - the same death can be an archive and a document of damage. Not the schedule either: a slot that disappears is not an archive if the object is still there, which is why the finale case separates grief from inconvenience.

Mechanism

Irreversibility, and the difference between the two things people grieve. One is a person who will not return; the other is a version - a performance, a lineup, a running gag, a platform era - which was always a construction and is now unavailable. The site's argument is that these have the same structure and deserve the same response, and the same study of a soap finale is the evidence.

Absorbs the old grief section. It was never an attachment target; it is what happens to one.

Contest

The tie is being argued about in public, usually by people who have never met. Fandom wars, backlash, being publicly wrong. The argument is about a stranger and is never really about the stranger.

Test

Is the argument happening in public about a person who is not in the room? If the disagreement is conducted among strangers with the object as its subject and not its participant, this is contest. The test is about who is arguing, not whether they are right.

Not

Not criticism. A critique of a work is addressed to whoever might read it; contest is addressed to the other side of a fight in which the object cannot answer. Not fandom, which has rules and can hold a member in good standing - the fandom body is a register and an attachment category at once, and confusing the two is how a conversation turns into a pile-on.

Mechanism

Identity maintenance in public. The argument is rarely about the stranger; it is a way of stating who the arguer is and who they are not, with the object as the occasion. Which is why the outcome arrives as a fact nobody chose: a pile-on produces a position nobody held a minute earlier, and the ratio entry names the weapon - attention, not truth.

Absorbs the old contest section.

Harm

The tie has produced a documented cost to somebody, whether that somebody consented to it or not. Distinct from PREDATOR, which is an attachment target: harm is a register that any of the eight can be in.

Test

Has somebody paid a documented cost? Not distress that the audience found impressive, and not discomfort - a cost, borne by a named party, that can be described without the word should. If the harm register applies, the claim has to survive being stated as an event rather than as a feeling.

Not

Not PREDATOR. This is the distinction the two axes exist to make: harm is a register that any of the eight attachment categories can be in, while PREDATOR is the one category where the object itself is a predator. A fandom can cause harm and is not a predator; a stalker is one regardless of how anyone feels about it. Conflating them makes the serious case sound like an accusation.

Mechanism

A one-sided tie that acquires an address. Harm happens where the attachment stops being private and starts acting on somebody - through a crowd, through a policy, through a door. The register is what any attachment can escalate into, which is the reason it is recorded separately rather than as a category, and the reason the site's own project is worth the risk of writing it down.

Absorbs the danger material by register rather than by category, while PREDATOR keeps the cases where the object itself is a predator.

PERSONA · 33 entries · 19 stories

The one who performs at you. A real person, doing a job, in front of you.

Real, and working. Which is what makes it complicated - the asymmetry is manufactured by somebody who is paid to produce it.

The idol 16

A performer at enough distance that the work is mostly finished before you arrive. Musicians, film stars, athletes, heads of state treated as performers rather than as office-holders. The tie is to a body of work and a public image that the person is not required to maintain for you.

Test

Did they make the thing, and is there a version of them that exists whether or not anyone is watching? If yes, this is an idol. If the work was assembled for them, they are still a persona but the manufacturing is the story - see the manufactured teen idol and the boy band.

Not

Not an athlete, where the tie is to a role that transfers between holders. Not a politician, where the tie is to an office. Not a fiction. The idol's distinguishing property is distance: the work is finished before you get there, so you are never present for the making.

Mechanism

Distance does the work. A finished body of work cannot be caught changing, and the person is not present to be contradicted. What the audience is attached to is a surface maintained by others - a label, a publicist, a tour - which is why the attachment survives the person and why the archive problem arrives when they go.

The daily creator 9

A person whose output is regular enough to have a place in your week, and who is visible in real time. Streamers, podcasters, video essayists, talk hosts. The tie is to the schedule before it is to the person.

Test

Can you say what day it is? If the attachment has a recurring slot attached to a person rather than to a programme, this is a creator. The test is deliberately about the schedule, because for this group the schedule is usually the more reliable half of the bond.

Not

Not a presenter, who is handed the direct address by an institution rather than generating it. Not a platform, which is the institution that profits from the schedule. A creator is a person; the moment the attachment is to the platform rather than the person, it moves to INSTITUTION.

Mechanism

Regularity plus visibility. A fixed slot builds the habit and real time lets the performer react to the room, which is the reciprocal edge - unevenly distributed, but present in a way television is not. The livestreaming literature frames this as one-and-a-half sided rather than one-sided, and that is the honest description.

The presenter 2

The one who addresses you directly, by name, in second person. Newsreaders, weathermen, game show hosts, telethon appeals. The purest case of Horton's direct address: the illusion is engineered, and it works because it is engineered.

Test

Did an institution put them there, and do they speak in the second person on a schedule? Direct address plus an employer is the test. It is also the reason this is the sub-category the field has measured longest and understood least.

Not

Not a creator, who generates their own address rather than being assigned it. Not a cleric, who addresses an assembly in person rather than an audience at a distance. The presenter's defining constraint is that the intimacy is a job function with a shift behind it.

Mechanism

Second-person grammar aimed at a large audience is a machine for manufacturing the feeling of being singled out. Horton and Wohl described it as a simulacrum of conversational give-and-take, and the key condition they identified was that it is triggered when a performer acknowledges the audience's presence. The perceived intimacy is not a side effect; it is the product.

The athlete 4

Attachment to performance rather than to a person. You follow the sport and the person arrives attached to it. Transfer, retirement and injury are the recurring breaks, which is why this sits so close to the archive register.

Test

Does the attachment survive the person leaving? If a fan's grief is for the role rather than the holder - and the locality usually grieves the role - this is the athlete. The distinguishing test is whether a change of player produces a bereavement or a transfer complaint.

Not

Not a club, which is an institution the attachment can be handed to intact. Not an idol, where the tie is to a finished body of work rather than to current performance. An athlete is the one persona whose object is in motion.

Mechanism

Form and narrative arrive packaged, and a person is available to receive them at the right moment. Because the performance is ongoing, the bond is renewed on a schedule rather than recalled on one - which is what makes a transfer feel like a betrayal rather than an ending.

  • Kobe Bryant2020 · An event or momentPublic mourning for an athlete that drew on the athlete's own stated bond with a young fan. The mourning is documented and the bond is stated rather than inferred, which makes it the cleanest athlete case on the site.
  • Nick Drake1948 · A personWrongly filed under a creator idiom. Three records, withdrew from touring, dead in 1974, and the argument about what he meant ran for fifty years. That is attachment to a person who withdrew from performance - the archive register, on a persona.
  • The athlete and the city1920 · A mechanism or tacticSupport for a team or an athlete as a bond with a person, a place and a lineage, frequently inherited from a parent. Three objects in one bond, which is why the sport sub-category exists separately from the idol one.
  • The transfer1900 · A mechanism or tacticA player attached to for years leaves, and the grievance is felt as betrayal by the entire locality. The clearest demonstration that the object was a role rather than a person, because the locality grieves at the role.
The head of state 2

Someone addressed as a collective rather than as an individual. The parasocial tie is to a role that outlives the person holding it, which is a different object from an idol's and breaks differently.

Test

Does the attachment survive an election? If the same warmth transfers intact from one office-holder to the next, the object was the role, not the person. That is the test, and it is also the finding.

Not

Not an idol, and the difference is the point. An idol is a person with an audience; a sovereign is an office with a person in it. The political parasocial is structured as a personal bond and transfers poorly, which is the documented finding and the reason it is its own sub-category.

Mechanism

The office supplies a stage and a script, and the person supplies a face the audience is willing to attach to. The two are separable, and the fact that they are separable is what makes the category distinct: the attachment is to a synthesis that will not survive its own terms.

  • Charisma as a measurable asset1968 · A study or paperWhy some candidates reliably produce personal attachment and others do not, at comparable policy positions. The tie is to a person holding an office, which is a different object from an idol's and breaks differently.
  • The political parasocial1960 · A mechanism or tacticSupport structured as a personal bond rather than a policy preference, transferring poorly across candidates. Proof that the tie is to the person, which is the same finding the Power Rangers handover documents from the other end.

The written character 17

Somebody described in a book and therefore available to an unlimited number of people at once, none of whom can be contradicted. The largest parasocial population on this site and the one with the longest memory.

Test

Is the figure available in more than one place at the same time, to people who have never met? If yes, and if no author can override what the audience says they feel, this is the written character. The second half of the test is what separates a character from a controlled property.

Not

Not a played role, where an actor can leave the costume and the audience notices. Not a mascot, which has no interior. The written character is the one with enough interior life that the audience fills it in and cannot be corrected.

Mechanism

A fictional interior is an invitation with no host present. The audience supplies continuity, motive and opinion, and because the text never closes the account the audience is never caught being wrong. The reader's side of it was described in Werther before anyone had a word for parasocial, and the fashion the novel caused is the first documented case of a mass attachment transferring from a page to a life.

The played role 4

An actor inside a character, where the audience's tie splits between the two and the split is itself the subject. Pee-wee Herman is the clean case: a performed self with a person performing it, and a show built on being able to switch it off.

Test

Can you name the actor and the character, and does the audience feel something different about each? If both names carry weight and they are not the same weight, the attachment has split, and the split is what this sub-category is for. A sequel with the same actor and a different reception is the decisive case.

Not

Not a written character, where the author is a name the audience rarely holds in the same hand. Not a virtual creator, where the persistent identity is the character's and the performer is hidden rather than visible. The played role requires the performer to be legible as a person.

Mechanism

Two attachments to one set of costumes, and an audience sophisticated enough to hold both. The Power Rangers handover is the cleanest demonstration on the site that the attachment can be transferred wholesale between holders of a costume - which means the object was never the person underneath it.

  • The sitcom family1960 · A mechanism or tacticA recurring family played by the same cast, five nights a week, for years, in a house you could draw from memory. Domestic fiction with a fixed cast, which is the played-role case without a single performer leaving.
  • The Power Rangers handover1993 · An event or momentA costume is put on by a new actor and the audience transfers its entire attachment to whoever is now inside it, five days a week, in front of millions of children, with nobody pretending otherwise. The cleanest proof that the object was never the person.

The myth 1

Figures predating any surviving text, carried by retelling. Pygmalion, and the long list of figures who were never written down and are not going out of fashion.

Test

Is there a text, and does the figure predate it or survive its loss? If the figure arrives by retelling rather than by reading, this is a myth figure rather than a written character. The absence of a canonical version is the tell.

Not

Not a written character, who has a fixed text that can be quoted against the audience's version. Not a deity inside a working belief system, which belongs in the divine category because the relationship there is addressed rather than retold. A myth figure is told about; a deity is prayed to.

Mechanism

No text means no correction. The figure is available to every retelling, which makes it unusually durable and unusually imprecise, and the imprecision is what lets a reader project onto it. Pygmalion is the reason this site exists by name, and the story is careful to treat the impossibility as the appeal rather than a defect to be corrected.

  • Pygmalion8 · A workThe sculptor who fell in love with his own work, and the story treats the impossibility as part of the appeal rather than a tragedy to be corrected. A myth figure, and the one that named the whole subject.
The mascot 3

A figure with no body, no biography and no intention: an emote, a logo-person, a face given a name. The purest parasocial object on the site, because nothing can disappoint it.

Test

Does the figure have an interior anybody could get wrong, or is it specified entirely by others? If a company's asset can be described without reference to its own feelings, and the audience supplies the interior, this is a mascot. The test is whether it can be the author of its own behaviour - and usually it cannot.

Not

Not a written character, who can contradict you. Not a companion AI, which can - that is the whole problem with the companion category and the reason it needs its own. A mascot is inert by construction, which is exactly what makes it the safest attachment available and why it is held by children.

Mechanism

A surface with no owner, available to everyone. The Saturday-morning letter is the purest available case: a child wrote to a magazine about a fictional brand of baby food, the brand never appeared, the letter was never answered, and the attachment continued anyway. Nothing can disappoint it because nothing can act.

A likeness with no continuity, reaching half the planet - the archive register performed as technology.
  • Gigi1943 · A workA song where the singer pleads to be given the exact shape a man wants. An imaginary person defined entirely by someone else's specification - the attachment target is an image with no agency, which is the mascot case.
  • The NEST letter1991 · An event or momentA child writes to a magazine about a fictional brand of baby food. The brand never appears, the letter is never answered, and the episode ends - an attachment to a mascot held by a five-year-old with no reciprocity available at all.
The historic as character 0

A real person who has been flattened into a type - the long-suffering husband, the tragic wife, the mad king - and is now met in the type rather than in the life. The flattening is the problem, not the person.

Test

Do you know the type, and would the real biography survive contact with it? If the person is encountered through a role they were assigned after they were dead, this is the historic as character. It is the sub-category with no entries on this site, and that absence is itself a finding: the type is more common in the culture than it is in this corpus.

Not

Not a person, which has an interior available to them. Not a myth figure, which never had one. The distinction that matters is that the person existed and the type was imposed, so every accurate detail is in tension with the attachment.

Mechanism

The type is cheap to carry and pre-installed, so a figure can be understood before any history is known. The cost is that the type is not available for correction and the person is not present to object, which is why this sub-category is named after the flattening rather than after the figure.

The stalker 3

The tie continued past the point where the other end could not consent, and the address becomes physical. Twenty-two visits and a hundred and thirty-three doorbell rings is a documented conviction, not a complaint.

Mechanism

The same mechanism as every other case, with a direction of travel added. From the ten sections to the eight categories

The ten sections are not the eight categories. The sections are how the news is filed; the categories are what a bond is to. Both are needed and neither substitutes for the other, which is why the front page has not been renumbered.

idol→persona, divine

A head of state was filed under idol because idol was the only slot for a famous person with a job. The new taxonomy separates the performer from the office.

creator→persona

Unchanged in substance, renamed to sit beside the other persona sub-categories rather than as a peer of them.

character→character

Unchanged, and now has five sub-categories instead of being a flat box.

companion→companion

Unchanged, and now includes the pre-LLM bots the old section had nowhere to put.

world→cosmology

Renamed. The old name suggested a place; the attachment is to a system of meaning, which is a different thing and a clearer one.

brand→institution

A brand is one kind of institution that learned your name. So are platforms, clubs, fandoms and congregations, and they were all being forced into one box.

crossover→officer

Renamed to name the actual asymmetry: a person with a duty toward you and a door. The old name described the fact of the case rather than what makes it different.

contest→predator, register:contest

Split. The part of it that is an argument about a stranger is a register and now lives on that axis. The part of it that is a fandom turning on one of its own is an in-group harm, which is an attachment target.

grief→register:archive

Moved wholesale off the category axis. Grief was never what the bond was to - it is what happened to it - and keeping it as a section is what made the old taxonomy impossible to extend.

danger→predator, register:harm

Split. Where the object of the attachment is itself a predator, it is a target: stalking, sasaeng, doxxing. Where the harm came out of an ordinary attachment, it is a register and applies to all eight.

Migrated on the taxonomy page: every one of the 175 encyclopedia entries and all 47 stories are placed on the new axes. The ten sections remain as the news filing, because a section is a place to look and a category is a thing to think about.
