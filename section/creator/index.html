A platform CEO and a 35-million-subscriber creator share one stage

VidSummit 2026 books the chief executive of one platform and one of the largest creators on YouTube onto the same stage. The event is publicly ticketed and creator-facing; nobody has been locked out of anything.

Two things were true at once in Dallas and only one of them was interesting enough to write down. The dull one: a creator economy conference is a business conference, it sells tickets, and the people who come are the people who make the money. Fine. That is what a trade fair is. The other one is on the speakers list. Dan Clancy, who runs Twitch, and SSSniperWolf, who has thirty-five million subscribers and has never once been on a Twitch stage, are billed at the same conference. The executive who sets the terms and the person who lives under them, in the same room, on the same programme. That is the shape of this whole thing, honestly. The people who are enormous and the people who own the enormous are not adversaries so much as co-signatories. Neither of them is in the room with the people watching. There was a version of this story where the fans were barred, and it would have been a better headline, and it is not true.

A streamer said his best friend of 25 years took $100,000. Fans said loyalty is dead

YourRAGE of FaZe. The money is the story. The best friend is the story.

Twenty-five years of friendship does not survive being a public commodity, and everybody involved is surprised. I have seen this exact pattern in every business I have worked in: the moment the audience becomes visible to both sides of a relationship, the relationship becomes work, and work has an accounting department. The fans in that thread were not being naive. They were watching a twenty-five-year friendship get invoiced in public.

CREATORLAW 2

Podcast listeners score suspiciously high on parasocial attachment

And so do people who like novelty and good conversation, which is most of us and a good deal less exotic than the discourse assumes.

Podcast listening is the single most parasocial habit in the English language and it is very rarely discussed as such, because it does not look like the stereotype. A host's voice in your ear twice a week for four years is a closer relationship than most people have with anyone who has not met them. This study is the first one I have seen that says so plainly.

CREATORLAW 2

Kai Cenat ended nine months of silence with a million people watching

Streamer University 2026, dual-platform, 120 creators, and the 'I Quit' pivot before it.

Nine months of nothing and a million people still turned up on the word. That is not an audience, that is a standing appointment, and standing appointments are the thing parasocial research keeps finding is the strongest predictor of anything. The Insight Company is about what happens when a business is built on the appearance of paying attention to you. The part I cannot resolve is how much of it was real, and how much had to be, to keep working.
