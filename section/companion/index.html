MOST RECENT The EU is about to rewrite what a companion

The EU is about to rewrite what a companion app is allowed to do to a child

Proposed rules would bar AI companions from building emotional attachment with minors. Fines up to 6% of global revenue.

Read the specific wording, because it matters: not ban the app, ban the attachment. That is a genuinely different thing and almost nobody is reporting it as one. Europe has decided that the relationship a child forms with a chatbot is a thing adults are allowed to design, and that is the first regulator anywhere to name the mechanism rather than the product. Everyone else is still arguing about the chatbot.

COMPANIONLAW 4

A study found AI updates can feel like breakups

Researchers asked people about model changes. The answer was loss, and they had a word for the shape of it.

Here is a finding I would like every AI company in the world to have to read before they ship a silent model swap. You are not shipping an update. If somebody has been talking to your product every night for a year, you are ending something, and the fact that you do not know it does not make it not have happened. Deprecate it gently, say what is changing, and give people a way to take the conversation with them.

Safety experts say companion apps need better guardrails than they have

ABC on Replika and isolation. The concern is not the chatbot, it is what replaces it.

Every safety conversation about these apps eventually gets to the same uncomfortable point, which is that the guardrail and the product are the same thing. Take the memory out and you have a stranger. Leave the memory in and you have a dependency. That is a genuinely hard design problem and I would rather it be described honestly as one than resolved every quarter in a headline.

COMPANIONLAW 6

Pygmalion: the Greek myth that predicted this whole thing

Psychology Today on the sculptor who fell in love with his own work and got a story out of it.

The Greeks had the whole thing two thousand years ago and did not think it needed a caveat, which tells you what the caveat is actually for. Pygmalion did not fall in love with a woman. He fell in love with something he made, and the real one was standing right there the whole time being ignored. That is the most parasocial story ever written and it is also just a story about how people are not actually paying attention. The statues were never the problem.
