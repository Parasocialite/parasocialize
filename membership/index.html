Two things I want to be straight about, because I know what this looks like from the outside.

One: the free part is genuinely free and I am not going to put the archive behind this in six months. If the site ever gets more popular, the thing that grows is the index, not the paywall.

Two: this is not a scheme to make you feel bad for not paying. Most of the people reading this will never renew and the site has to work for them anyway, because that is the deal. Six dollars is for the people who read it every day.

What you would actually be buying

Six dollars buys you a filing cabinet nobody else has.

The reason this can't be scraped is not the writing. It is the structure. Ten sections that hold idol culture and courtrooms and the teachers in my classroom. Seven laws that every card is filed against. A filter I run by hand against fifteen feeds, which files what it finds and leaves the rest in a queue I work through. And a shelf of novels, albums and videos that each card leans on when it needs to make a point. None of that is a blog post. It is an instrument, and I built it, and it is the only one.

WHAT YOU GET 01

Cross-referenced, not chronological

Every card points at its section, its law, and the piece of my work that got there first. Follow a law across four hundred cards and you get a shape that a reverse-chronological feed can never show you.

WHAT YOU GET 02

The queue too

Right now the filter has turned up sixty-odd things I have not ruled on, and it throws nothing away on its own — every one of them is waiting on me. Those are editorial decisions a human should be able to audit, and right now only I can see them. You would be able to as well. Nothing has been rejected yet, and I would rather say that than imply there is a pile of rejections waiting for you.

WHAT YOU GET 03

Yours to keep

The whole thing as JSON. If I stop, you still have it. That is not a retention tactic, it is what happens when you are a small site run by one person who has had things go offline before.
