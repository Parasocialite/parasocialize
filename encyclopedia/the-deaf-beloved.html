A work · 370 BC · Antiquity

Shag-eared and deaf

Plato describes an older man in love with a boy who is compared, in the text, to a hunting animal that is deaf. The animal's ears are the point: the love is addressed to someone who cannot hear it, and the whole scene turns on that.

Applicationliterature

The earliest surviving description of this site's subject written down by someone who was inside the institution rather than looking at it from outside, and it is startling how modern the anatomy is. In the Phaedrus the lover hunts a beast that is 'shag-eared and deaf', and the closing image is the one nobody expects: having finally got what he chased, the lover finds himself running away and the *beast* pursuing him, because it never understood the terms. The one-sided party was not withholding. It was deaf. And in the First Alcibiades, Alcibiades tells Socrates that he is the only lover who has not abandoned the suit and that the others 'pestered you with their conversation' while he stayed silent for years - so the same structure is described from both ends, which almost nothing else on this site manages. It is also the first entry here where the power asymmetry is institutional rather than accidental, and the first where a written text tells you that role reversals were constant: the beloved could become the lover. Whether that is friendship or something else was never settled, which is why it belongs here.

Application. One-sided attachment put to work on purpose. Creator economies, community, fandom as infrastructure, and the things that genuinely help people. Not automatically good - a tool with an obvious use is a tool that will also get a bad one.
