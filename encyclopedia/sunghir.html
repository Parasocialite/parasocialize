A work · 34000 BC · Deep prehistory

Beads, and one bull

At Sunghir on the Russian plain, thirty-four thousand years ago, three burials were lavish with beads and ochre and one was not treated that way at all. People were buried in a site that kept producing them for thousands of years, individually and very differently.

Applicationhistory

This entry exists to disagree with another one on this site. Shanidar is the earliest evidence of people returning to the dead; Sunghir is the evidence that this was not one practice with one belief behind it. Of the individuals recovered, four were intentionally buried, two had their remains manipulated and one seems to have received little or no formal treatment, and the only thing uniting the three rich burials is the quantity of beads - which the analysis reads as social behaviour in life rather than in death. That is a harder finding than a uniform origin would be, and it is better for this site, because it means the archaeological record cannot be read as a single relationship any more than the later one can. Formicola's case is the memorable one: at Romito Cave an adolescent dwarf was buried together with a woman, underneath an engraving of a bull. A named dead person, put under a named animal, deliberately, thirty thousand years before anything was written down.
