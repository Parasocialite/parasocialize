Top 8: The First Person We Kept

Before media, people still kept the dead close and spoke to figures who could not reply. This list is about the oldest evidence of a one-sided bond.

The oldest evidence that a relationship can be one-sided. Ranked by how much of it survives in the ground.

The site's argument is not modern. The eight below are the archaeological and early textual record: a body carried into a cave, a face carved in ivory, a mask walked through a funeral, a beloved who never learned the terms. The bond is older than the vocabulary.

  1. 1

    The dead kept in the cave

    History · 70000 BC

    Neanderthals repeatedly carried their dead into one cave in northern Iraq and put them down in the floor.

    Read the entry →
  2. 2

    Eleven thousand years of pillars

    History · 9500 BC–8200 BC

    Round enclosures on a hillside in Anatolia holding T-shaped pillars carved to suggest human figures, built by people who had not yet domesticated a single animal - used for over a thousand years, then deliberately filled in and built over.

    Read the entry →
  3. 3

    The carved animals

    History · 40000 BC

    Upper Paleolithic carvers in Europe made small figures of animals and people from mammoth ivory from about forty thousand years ago.

    Read the entry →
  4. 4

    The craft of the sage Adapa

    History · 660 BC

    A tablet from Nineveh written in the voice of Ashurbanipal, listing what he had been taught and by whom - a named sage he never met - and then what he did with it.

    Read the entry →
  5. 5

    Pygmalion

    History · 8

    The sculptor who fell in love with his own work. The statue was never going to be able to leave, and the story treats that as part of the appeal rather than a tragedy to be corrected.

    Read the entry →
  6. 6

    The story told again

    History · 800 BC

    Myths are not stories people tell once. They are stories with a fixed shape that get told to the same audience on a schedule.

    Read the entry →
  7. 7

    Eight thousand faces, all facing outward

    History · 210 BC

    Eight thousand life-size soldiers, each with a face and ears no other has, buried in battle order facing the enemy with the emperor behind them - made from the likenesses of real men so the substitute army would stand where the real one cou.

    Read the entry →
  8. 8

    Shag-eared and deaf

    Literature · 370 BC

    Plato describes an older man in love with a boy who is compared, in the text, to a hunting animal that is deaf. The animal's ears are the point: the love is addressed to someone who cannot hear it, and the whole scene turns on that.

    Read the entry →
