Top 8: The Fourth Wall Is a Lie

The fourth wall is the invisible line between a stage and its audience. This list is about the moment a performer steps through it, and what that does to a room that cannot answer back.

The theatre is where the audience is most present and least able to reply. Ranked by how deliberately the performance used that.

On stage the actor can see the audience and the audience can see the actor, which makes the one-sidedness stranger rather than smaller. The eight below are the record of what happens when the person who cannot answer is standing ten feet away.

  1. 1

    The tape could not answer

    Stage · 1958

    A man on his sixty-ninth birthday plays a recording he made at thirty-nine, and has the conversation the tape cannot have back.

    Read the entry →
  2. 2

    If you believe, clap your hands

    Stage · 1904

    The play that asks the whole audience to save a dying fairy by clapping - and has received the same answer, in the same room, for more than a century.

    Read the entry →
  3. 3

    The Divine Sarah

    Stage / Film · 1891

    Sarah Bernhardt toured more like a head of state than a player: when she sailed for New York in 1891 the crossing was covered the way a royal visit is, and 'Divine Sarah' was by then the whole public's phrase for her.

    Read the entry →
  4. 4

    The audience became the show

    Stage / Film · 1976

    Launched as a small London stage musical in 1973, filmed in 1975, and from 1976 screened at midnight before a room that shouted back: The Rocky Horror Picture Show ran while its audience performed it, wearing the costumes.

    Read the entry →
  5. 5

    Vote for the murderer

    Stage · 1985

    A musical of Dickens' unfinished novel that stops where the novel stops and turns the cliffhanger over to the seats: the audience votes who murdered Edwin Drood, who the disguised Dick Datchery is and which two characters end up together.

    Read the entry →
  6. 6

    A slightly more awkward version of himself

    Scripted television / Unscripted television · 2013

    A comedian who stages real situations for people who have not read the script, and performs a slightly more awkward version of himself inside them - the manufacturing is the show, and the audience has been told so from the beginning.

    Read the entry →
  7. 7

    The wall exists to keep you out

    Film / Literature · 1758

    Diderot invented the wall in 1758 for the purpose of stopping the audience noticing. Every breach since - a wink, a clap, a lecture - is that convention failing on purpose, and it is the only time the far side admits you are there.

    Read the entry →
  8. 8

    They killed her in the papers

    Film · 1910

    The Biograph Girl was reported killed under a streetcar, publicly resurrected by the producer who had just hired her, and brought to St. Louis to be mobbed in person - proof that an audience's grief could be produced on schedule.
