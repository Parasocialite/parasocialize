Top 8: The Friend Your Parents Bought

Children form these bonds before they can consent to them. This list is about the toys and shows sold to that fact.

The audience that cannot consent, and the industry that knows it. Ranked by how much of the relationship was engineered before the child could read.

Every mechanism on this site looks different when the person on the near side is six years old. The eight below are the record of what childhood fandom built: the puppet, the mascot, the club, the letter, the toy that moved the bond into the child's own hands.

  1. 1

    Say, kids, what time is it?

    Unscripted television · 1947–1960

    The first television character to be answered by name. Children three to eight shouted its name back at the screen every afternoon for thirteen years, and the puppet it was played by was recorded before the broadcast.

    Read the entry →
  2. 2

    But the beaver resists

    Film · 2011

    A depressed man finds a beaver hand puppet in the trash and can speak to his family only as the beaver; the arrangement repairs the family, survives the discovery that it was built on a lie, and removing it costs him an arm.

    Read the entry →
  3. 3

    Sixty-five percent by seven

    History · 1934

    A named, described character with an air of reality, kept going by being referred to. Two-thirds of children have one, most of them invisible, and a quarter of those are unknown to their parents.

    Read the entry →
  4. 4

    Won't you be my neighbor?

    Scripted television · 1968

    Fred Rogers went national on public television on 19 February 1968 and spent thirty-three years talking, slowly, into the camera at children aged two to five.

    Read the entry →
  5. 5

    Will you help me?

    Scripted television · 1996

    Blue's Clues premiered in 1996 and built its method out of a pause: the host looks into the camera, asks the child a question, waits through a silence written into the script, and then behaves as though an answer arrived.

    Read the entry →
  6. 6

    The Power Rangers handover

    Scripted television · 1993

    A costume is put on by a new actor and the audience transfers its entire attachment to whoever is now inside it. Running five days a week, in front of millions of children, with nobody pretending otherwise.

    Read the entry →
  7. 7

    The confessional that became a convention

    Unscripted television · 1992

    In 1992 MTV's The Real World gave reality television its least-questioned convention: a person alone in a room, speaking to a camera about what everyone just saw.

    Read the entry →
  8. 8

    The first superhero

    History · 1938

    A character who cannot die, printed in a newspaper, read by a generation of children on the same day.
