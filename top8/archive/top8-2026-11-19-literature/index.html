Top 8: The Voice That Waits in the Book

A book is the slowest reply and the longest relationship. These are the moments reading became correspondence.

The book is the slowest reply and the longest relationship. Ranked by how completely the reader supplied the other end.

A novel can be put down and picked up for years, and the figure inside it never changes while the reader does. The eight below are the record of what that does: the character who becomes a companion, the author who becomes a face, the diary addressed to somebody who cannot write back.

  1. 1

    Virtue rewarded

    Literature · 1740

    The novel that turned its readers into a correspondent. Pamela Andrews writes home about her master's pursuit, and the reader takes the seat of the parents - receiving every letter, sending none.

    Read the entry →
  2. 2

    Johann Wolfgang von Goethe

    Literature · 1749–1832

    Author of Werther, and of the observation that people keep making the same mistakes, which is arguably the first great novel to describe a reader being changed by a book.

    Read the entry →
  3. 3

    Werther

    Literature · 1774

    The young man of Goethe's novel who reads too much, loves a woman who does not love him back, and kills himself, in a book that made that behaviour fashionable across Europe for a decade.

    Read the entry →
  4. 4

    Miguel de Cervantes

    Literature · 1547–1616

    Author of Don Quixote, and of a narrator who keeps having to explain away the reality of what his character is doing.

    Read the entry →
  5. 5

    Cordially yours, Betty Crocker

    Literature / Radio · 1921

    In 1921 a flour company's advertising department needed one woman's name for the kitchen advice its all-female home-service staff was already writing.

    Read the entry →
  6. 6

    Am I really like that?

    Literature · 1890

    The painter stands in front of the figure he made and asks whether the man is really like it. He is. And it will never alter, which is the only thing about it anybody wanted.

    Read the entry →
  7. 7

    Don Quixote

    Literature · 1605

    An old man who reads so many books about chivalry that he goes out to live as a knight, against all evidence.

    Read the entry →
  8. 8

    The reply that was printed

    Comics · 1962

    From 1962 Stan Lee ran fan mail in the back of The Fantastic Four and answered it in print in his own voice, so a reader could see their name answered on a page.
