Two women died in the same month and the discourse is about whether you are allowed to be sad

Dolly Parton and Gloria Steinem. One sang, one argued. Grief treated them identically and got accused of not knowing the difference.

FILED 26 SEP 2026 · 2d ago ·Due back 26 OCT 2026 ·BY YAHOO LIFESTYLE AUSTRALIA

Here is a test I would put to anyone who finds the parasocial grief coverage embarrassing: would you say the same thing if the person who died were a friend of yours? Most people would not, and that inconsistency is the entire problem with the discourse. Grief does not check your credentials first. If you sang along to nine and a half million people, you are allowed to be sad about her.

Notice, name, choose. That's the whole method.

GRIEFYesterdayLAW 4

When a famous person dies it can feel like losing someone you loved

The Guardian ran this on the exact week Dolly Parton, Gloria Steinem and Liam Payne died. The timing is not the story. The timing proves the story.

I want to push back on the word distress in every piece that runs this week, because it is doing too much work. Losing somebody is what happens when the people you love die. If a stadium full of people are grieving Dolly Parton, that is not a malfunction, that is a very large number of people finding out that they loved her. The only unusual thing about parasocial grief is that the person could not have known you were there. That is worth being careful with. It is not worth being condescending about.

A woman died in Seoul and by the next day the argument was about whether the fandom was toxic

The pivot from a death to a discourse about ourselves, in about a day, in front of a diaspora that had to watch it happen.

A person died and the discussion moved to the conduct of the people who were sad about her, which is a way of making the loss about us again. Grief needs witnesses and the argument made sure there were none left standing. All The Horrible Things exists for exactly this hour: media we loved that did something unforgivable, kept anyway, because keeping it is part of how you get through it. There is no tidy version of this and there should not be one.
