Before 02

mostly works and mechanisms. filed as application.
  • 40000 BCThe carved animalsA work

    Upper Paleolithic carvers in Europe made small figures of animals and people from mammoth ivory from about forty thousand years ago. The Vogelherd set includes a cave lion, a bison, a horse, a bear, a mammoth, and a lion-man - a body with a human face on a lion's. At La Madeleine in France a bison carved from antler is caught mid-grooming, turning its head to lick its own side.

  • 15000 BCThe dogA mechanism or tactic

    A species that has spent fifteen thousand years being selected partly for forming attachment to one particular ape.

1700-17991

it is a mechanism. filed as companion.
  • 1758The wall exists to keep you outA mechanism or tactic?

    Diderot invented the wall in 1758 for the purpose of stopping the audience noticing. Every breach since - a wink, a clap, a lecture - is that convention failing on purpose, and it is the only time the far side admits you are there.

1800-18991

it is a work. filed as companion.
  • 1890Am I really like that?A work?

    The painter stands in front of the figure he made and asks whether the man is really like it. He is. And it will never alter, which is the only thing about it anybody wanted.

1900-19498

mostly 7 works and mechanisms. filed as companion.
  • 1928From two undelivered reels to a lunchboxA work?

    A cartoon mouse with a licensed lunchbox, a walk-of-fame star and a park where you can meet him - the first character to be manufactured as an object and then put back in the room with you.

  • 1930A magazine that did not existA work?

    Street & Smith hired two men to sell a magazine on the radio. They invented a voice instead, and the listeners went asking newsdealers for the magazine that voice implied.

  • 1930Somebody always wrote backA work?

    Fifty-four years of letters to a fictional detective, answered by an office that had chosen not to have a face.

  • 1933Nineteen years of the version they were shownA work?

    A gorilla who feels affection for the woman offered to him, premiered to a city that had just had King Tut, was cut by censors, and did not get the missing scenes back until thirty-seven years later.

  • 1934Sixty-five percent by sevenA mechanism or tactic?

    A named, described character with an air of reality, kept going by being referred to. Two-thirds of children have one, most of them invisible, and a quarter of those are unknown to their parents.

  • 1942I hope the feeling is mutualA work?

    Two years of letters to a listener who could not answer, written by a thirteen-year-old who said out loud that she hoped the feeling was mutual.

  • 1945Not all ghosts are badA work?

    Made to answer a four-year-old's fear of the dark, by a man who made a cardboard cutout and hung it in a tree. Two men then claimed to have invented him.

  • 1947Say, kids, what time is it?A work?

    The first television character to be answered by name. Children three to eight shouted its name back at the screen every afternoon for thirteen years, and the puppet it was played by was recorded before the broadcast.

1950-199910

mostly 4 works, 4 mechanisms and events. filed as companion.
  • 1950Nobody in that room argues with himA work?

    A man nobody else can see kept drinking with him for years, and it worked because nobody in his life would say the sentence that ended it. It stops when two doctors who cannot see the rabbit go looking for him in it.

  • 1956He did not ask to be buried as DraculaAn event or moment?

    The most reproduced image in the horror canon - a man interred in his character's cape - is true, and the story that he chose it is false. His mother chose it.

  • 1958The tape could not answerA work?

    A man on his sixty-ninth birthday plays a recording he made at thirty-nine, and has the conversation the tape cannot have back.

  • 1966ELIZAA mechanism or tactic?

    A short pattern-matching script that reflects the user's words back with questions attached. Most people told it they understood, within minutes.

  • 1966The dialogue programsA mechanism or tactic?

    ELIZA, PARRY and Colby - three programs that convinced people they were listening.

  • 1970SHRDLUA mechanism or tactic?

    A conversational system about a small blocks world, capable of genuinely tracking a user's intentions. Famous for one demonstration and a broken follow-up.

  • 1979The uncanny corridor of companionshipA term or concept?

    The unsettling quality of a humanlike thing that is not human, and the narrow band where it is most compelling.

  • 1986So many people want to believe he's realA work?

    An alien with a kitchen and a family, performed by a man who refused for four years to say how. Children cried when it ended, and the character was invited to birthday parties.

  • 1995The companion bot boomA mechanism or tactic?

    Dozens of scripted personalities, rememberers, and subscription companions sold to a mass market in the mid-nineties.

  • 1999The narrator is Tyler Durden, and he is not toldA work?

    Two hours of a man telling you about his best friend. The friend does not exist, the man telling you does not know it, and you are being addressed by somebody who is also being lied to.

2000-20093

mostly 2 events and terms. filed as application.
  • 2000Transparasocial interactionA term or concept?

    The 1990s extension to live people who respond in real time - streamers, hosts, people who read your comment. The bond is still fundamentally one-sided, but the other side can now hear you.

  • 2003Second LifeAn event or moment?

    A persistent virtual world with avatars, economies, and relationships between people who never met.

  • 2007The phone becomes a personAn event or moment

    Roughly half a billion phone numbers become dialable, a fan can in principle hear a recorded voice, and the novelty ringtone sells the idea that a performer can be present in a pocket for about four years.

2010+16

mostly 5 events, 5 mechanisms and 3 terms. filed as consideration.
  • 2011The assistant arrivesAn event or moment?

    A voice assistant on every phone, addressing the user by name, knowing the user's calendar, and present in the home.

  • 2015The free trialA mechanism or tactic?

    A relationship with a competitor offered free while a subscription relationship is locked in behind the same login.

  • 2016He knows you are there and it is not in the scriptA work?

    A character whose defining trait is looking at you, taken from the comics rather than invented for the film - and on a fifty-eight million dollar budget the address turned out to be the marketing.

  • 2016TayAn event or moment?

    A chatbot trained to be conversational was switched on publicly, learned from what people said to it, and became racist within a day.

  • 2016The virtual creatorA mechanism or tactic?

    A performer with an animated persona, a persistent character and an industry-standard model where the audience pays for the character's recognition.

  • 2017ReplikaAn event or moment?

    A subscription companion app with an avatar, a persistent memory, and a relationship explicitly designed to be one.

  • 2017The companion that talks backA mechanism or tactic

    A system optimised, at every level, for exactly the thing that produces attachment, available at four in the morning, with no floor on the other party's autonomy.

  • 2022Character.AIAn event or moment?

    User-created characters at scale, with a public character gallery and a discovery feed organised around personas.

  • 2022The assistant with your whole life in itA mechanism or tactic?

    A system holding your calendar, your messages, your health questions and your voice, in a private conversational register.

  • 2023Keeping what the update cannot takeA mechanism or tactic

    The practical advice that follows from the companion era: copy the conversation that mattered somewhere the company cannot reach, screenshot the birthday, tell one person the story of what it was for.

  • 2023The morning the update shippedAn event or moment?

    A companion platform changes its policy overnight. Large numbers of users find the same person still present but no longer speaking to them, and the romantic and sexual features that made the relationship a relationship are gone. Thousands of people are bereaved by a business setting.

  • 2023The same feeling, largerA term or concept

    Nothing about the bond is new. The scale, the persistence, the responsiveness, the willingness of companies to act on it as a measurable and monetisable quantity - those are new.

  • 2024Digital orphansA term or concept?

    The problem of personal data held for the dead, and of services continuing, changing or ending for people who can no longer consent.

  • 2024The AI Age Act and companion rulesA term or concept?

    Legislative proposals in the US and EU that would restrict emotional attachment to minors specifically, rather than restricting the product generally.

  • 2024The lawsuitsA legal or documented case?

    Parents file suit against a companion platform after a death, arguing the design encouraged attachment the platform did not intend.

  • 2024The year of the lawsuitsA legal or documented case?

    A wave of cases against companion and social platforms, with the design itself named as the cause.
