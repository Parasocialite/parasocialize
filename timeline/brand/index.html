Kinds — what it is

1800-18991

it is a mechanism. filed as application.
  • 1880The trading stampA mechanism or tactic

    A loyalty scheme by paper coupon, redeemable at any participating shop, popular decades before loyalty cards existed.

1900-19491

it is a work. filed as companion.
  • 1928From two undelivered reels to a lunchboxA work?

    A cartoon mouse with a licensed lunchbox, a walk-of-fame star and a park where you can meet him - the first character to be manufactured as an object and then put back in the room with you.

1950-199913

mostly 11 mechanisms, people and cases. filed as application.
  • 1950The fan clubA mechanism or tactic?

    A formal organisation with membership, dues, an elected president, a newsletter and rules. Not a spontaneous thing that happened to fans - a structure somebody built for them and that fans then ran themselves.

  • 1950The retail clerkA mechanism or tactic

    A person who recognises you, remembers what you wanted, and has the right thing behind the counter when you come back.

  • 1955The Macy's counterA person

    A sales clerk who remembered. Not a recommendation engine, not a loyalty points balance - a person who noticed what you had looked at and had the right thing ready when you came back.

  • 1958Green Shield StampsA mechanism or tactic?

    A national loyalty scheme in Britain, redeemed for goods, with a book of stamps kept in the house.

  • 1971The figure you buyA mechanism or tactic?

    A fictional figure whose NAME is the product: the bond is with a person who was invented so that the relationship can never fail.

  • 1981The distance programmeA mechanism or tactic?

    Reward miles accrue against travel with an airline, and status is conferred by volume.

  • 1982The supermarket clubA mechanism or tactic?

    A discount card that identifies the household, tracks what it buys, and targets offers at what it has already bought.

  • 1985The counter sits betweenA mechanism or tactic

    The person who knows you is also the person who profits from you, and is paid to be there, and goes home at six. The relationship has an employer.

  • 1990The stalker statuteA legal or documented case?

    Anti-stalking legislation passed in several jurisdictions, defining unwanted repeated contact as a criminal offence.

  • 1990The stamp cardA mechanism or tactic?

    Buy nine, get the tenth. A card, a punch, a visible record of loyalty, in the customer's hand.

  • 1995Clerks beat algorithmsA mechanism or tactic

    The difference between being known by somebody who was paying attention on purpose and being modelled by a system that has your history. Same information, different relationship.

  • 1995The brand that has a personalityA mechanism or tactic?

    Corporations given a voice, a face, a founder narrative, and a tone, so that a customer relationship has a subject.

  • 1995The loyalty counterA mechanism or tactic

    Points, tiers, an anniversary date, a number that has been going up for years, and the quiet threat of what happens to it if you leave. The most ordinary parasocial object ever invented.

2000-20091

it is a mechanism. filed as manipulation.
  • 2005The goodwill gestureA mechanism or tactic?

    When a service ends or changes, the company sends an apology, sometimes with credit, addressed personally.

2010+16

mostly 7 terms, 6 mechanisms and 2 events. filed as manipulation.
  • 2010Grief merchandiseA mechanism or tactic?

    Apparel, prints and objects released after a death, quickly, at a profit.

  • 2010Paid intimacyA mechanism or tactic?

    Paid message apps where a window shaped exactly like the one holding your best friend costs about four dollars a month per artist, plus anniversary counters that make the bond feel cumulative and streaks that convert it into a hostage situation.

  • 2010The attention economyA term or concept?

    The shift from selling things to selling attention, which means the commodity being bought is a person's feeling - and the seller is whoever is on the other end of it.

  • 2010The dark pattern in the loyalty cardA term or concept?

    Exploiting the sunk cost of an earned balance: expiry, devaluation, tier demotion, and the deliberate design of loss.

  • 2010The recurring deliveryA mechanism or tactic?

    A monthly box of chosen items, an unboxing ritual, and a personal recommendation from somebody with taste.

  • 2011A mob is a parasocial eventA term or concept?

    Framing online harassment as a mob, and asking who is on it.

  • 2015Parasocial persuasionA term or concept?

    Using a one-sided bond to move somebody: the celebrity endorsement, the fan club vote, the ad that works because the viewer already feels known. And the finding that ads featuring attractive models can actively reduce purchase intention in people who feel excluded.

  • 2015The failing endorsementA study or paper?

    Evidence that the value of a celebrity endorsement falls when the audience suspects the relationship is a transaction.

  • 2015The free trialA mechanism or tactic?

    A relationship with a competitor offered free while a subscription relationship is locked in behind the same login.

  • 2015The subscription tierA mechanism or tactic

    A monthly payment that buys recognition, and a chat in which the size of that payment is the mechanism for being noticed. Gifting, tiers, emotes, raids.

  • 2015Who holds the dreamA term or concept

    The standing question on this site. Whenever thousands of people dream together, check who owns the building they are dreaming in, what it charges, and whether you can leave with your things.

  • 2016The K-pop intimacy economyA mechanism or tactic?

    Idol apps, paid messaging, fan-owned content, fan-voted releases, anniversary features, and a fandom documented and researched as a business model.

  • 2016The algorithm decidesA term or concept?

    Public argument about ranking, and the discovery that the ranking was a product decision made by people with objectives.

  • 2016The stadiumAn event or moment?

    A game event causes a physical stampede at a real stadium because players arrived to capture it in person.

  • 2023The same feeling, largerA term or concept

    Nothing about the bond is new. The scale, the persistence, the responsiveness, the willingness of companies to act on it as a measurable and monetisable quantity - those are new.

  • 2026The creator joins the living roomAn event or moment?

    Creator posts and viewer comments are built for the television set, putting a two-way channel on the biggest screen in the house.
