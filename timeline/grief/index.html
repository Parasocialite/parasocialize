Before 03

all 3 of them are works. filed as application.
  • 70000 BCThe dead kept in the caveA work

    Neanderthals repeatedly carried their dead into one cave in northern Iraq and put them down in the floor. Shanidar 4 was found on his side with his arm under his head; Shanidar Z lay in a hollow gouged out to fit her, with a stone under her head. The cluster dates to roughly seventy to seventy-five thousand years ago.

  • 34000 BCBeads, and one bullA work

    At Sunghir on the Russian plain, thirty-four thousand years ago, three burials were lavish with beads and ochre and one was not treated that way at all. People were buried in a site that kept producing them for thousands of years, individually and very differently.

  • 250 BCThe wax masks in the hallA work

    A Roman aristocrat had a wax mask of his own face taken while he was alive - usually in his thirties or forties, on taking office - and it was kept in a cupboard in the entrance hall beside a painted family tree and a label listing his offices. He never saw it worn. After his death, actors put it on and walked it in his funeral procession, in order of death, wearing the robes of the office he had held.

mostly 3 events, 3 mechanisms and 2 works. filed as grief.
  • 1956He did not ask to be buried as DraculaAn event or moment?

    The most reproduced image in the horror canon - a man interred in his character's cape - is true, and the story that he chose it is false. His mother chose it.

  • 1958The tape could not answerA work?

    A man on his sixty-ninth birthday plays a recording he made at thirty-nine, and has the conversation the tape cannot have back.

  • 1962Fifty copies of a faceA work?

    Warhol's 1962 double panel repeats one publicity still of Marilyn Monroe fifty times: colour on the left, draining to monochrome on the right, taken from Gene Kornman's Niagara photograph. Monroe died in August of that year, and the silkscreen turns her into a pattern a person can look at without her.

  • 1970Grieving a person you never metA mechanism or tactic

    Collective mourning of a public figure, and the persistent argument about whether it counts. Measured in studies of soap opera finales finding grief markers the same shape as the end of a relationship.

  • 1980The finale studyA study or paper?

    Academic work measuring grief responses to soap opera finales and finding the same markers as bereavement.

  • 1983M*A*S*HAn event or moment?

    Twenty-eight million people watched a television finale and the largest audience for a broadcast in history.

  • 1990Mourning a person you metA term or concept?

    A working vocabulary for grief over a public figure, drawn from bereavement practice, used without qualification.

  • 1990The comfort rewatchA mechanism or tactic?

    The show you have already seen, watched again, not for the plot. Familiarity as a nervous system intervention.

  • 1994Kurt CobainAn event or moment?

    A death broadcast in real time to a generation that had grown up with a songwriter's complaints as a soundtrack.

  • 1994The character deathA mechanism or tactic?

    Killing a popular character for narrative effect, and the organised reaction that follows.
