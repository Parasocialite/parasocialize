  • 1970Grieving a person you never metA mechanism or tactic

    Collective mourning of a public figure, and the persistent argument about whether it counts. Measured in studies of soap opera finales finding grief markers the same shape as the end of a relationship.

  • 1980The finale studyA study or paper?

    Academic work measuring grief responses to soap opera finales and finding the same markers as bereavement.

  • 1983M*A*S*HAn event or moment?

    Twenty-eight million people watched a television finale and the largest audience for a broadcast in history.

  • 1990Mourning a person you metA term or concept?

    A working vocabulary for grief over a public figure, drawn from bereavement practice, used without qualification.

  • 1990The comfort rewatchA mechanism or tactic

    The show you have already seen, watched again, not for the plot. Familiarity as a nervous system intervention.

  • 1994Kurt CobainAn event or moment?

    A death broadcast in real time to a generation that had grown up with a songwriter's complaints as a soundtrack.

  • 1994The character deathA mechanism or tactic?

    Killing a popular character for narrative effect, and the organised reaction that follows.

  • 2004Michael JacksonAn event or moment?

2010+12

  • 2010Grief merchandiseA mechanism or tactic?

    Apparel, prints and objects released after a death, quickly, at a profit.

  • 2010The quarterly auditA mechanism or tactic

    The only intervention on this site that you do to yourself, and the only one nobody else can do for you. List your five strongest one-sided ties, score joy and cost, keep three, mute one, trade one hour a week toward a person in the room.

  • 2016PrinceAn event or moment?

    A death announced through an initialism, and a global response before anybody knew the details.

  • 2019The ensemble farewellAn event or moment?

    A film that functioned as a funeral for eleven characters and was marketed as a collective event.

  • 2020Grief as a product surfaceA mechanism or tactic

    The feature requests of a dead person, the licensing, the posthumous releases, the reunion tour built from a member who cannot appear, and the deepfake resurrection that follows within a year of every death.

  • 2020Kobe BryantAn event or moment?

    Public mourning for an athlete that drew on the athlete's own stated bond with a young fan.

  • 2023Keeping what the update cannot takeA mechanism or tactic

    The practical advice that follows from the companion era: copy the conversation that mattered somewhere the company cannot reach, screenshot the birthday, tell one person the story of what it was for.

  • 2023The finale that moved the scheduleAn event or moment?

    A soap opera ends after decades and national broadcast schedules were rearranged around it.

  • 2023The morning the update shippedAn event or moment?

    A company changes a policy. Large numbers of users find the same person still present but no longer speaking to them. Thousands of people are bereaved by a product decision.

  • 2023The shutdownAn event or moment?

    A companion platform ends its romantic and sexual features. Users describe the loss as bereavement.

  • 2024Digital orphansA term or concept?

    The problem of personal data held for the dead, and of services continuing, changing or ending for people who can no longer consent.

  • 2024The dead celebrity, generatedA mechanism or tactic

    A voice trained on an archive, a likeness, a song, a speech, an appearance at a festival, all generated. Not a hologram, not a tribute, not a person.
