IDOL

The one who performs at you. Musicians, actors, athletes, heads of state.

Lanes — what it does

Kinds — what it is

Before 02

all 2 of them are works. filed as application.
  • 70000 BCThe dead kept in the caveA work

    Neanderthals repeatedly carried their dead into one cave in northern Iraq and put them down in the floor. Shanidar 4 was found on his side with his arm under his head; Shanidar Z lay in a hollow gouged out to fit her, with a stone under her head. The cluster dates to roughly seventy to seventy-five thousand years ago.

  • 250 BCThe wax masks in the hallA work

    A Roman aristocrat had a wax mask of his own face taken while he was alive - usually in his thirties or forties, on taking office - and it was kept in a cupboard in the entrance hall beside a painted family tree and a label listing his offices. He never saw it worn. After his death, actors put it on and walked it in his funeral procession, in order of death, wearing the robes of the office he had held.

1800-18991

it is a mechanism. filed as application.
  • 1880Identification with a teamA mechanism or tactic

    Supporting a club as a condition of belonging to a place, a class, a family, or a city.

1900-19496

mostly 4 mechanisms and 2 events. filed as application.
  • 1900The coachA mechanism or tactic?

    An adult with authority over a child for years, who is remembered partly by how they made them feel at nine.

  • 1900The transferA mechanism or tactic?

    A player who has been attached to for years leaves, and the grievance is felt as betrayal by the entire locality.

  • 1920The athlete and the cityA mechanism or tactic

    Support for a team or an athlete as a bond with a person, a place and a lineage, frequently inherited from a parent.

  • 1920The fan mail departmentA mechanism or tactic?

    A physical object that had to be opened, a volume small enough for one person to hold, and a reply that was an exception. Clara Bow, Shirley Temple, Judy Garland, all had staff whose job was correspondence. Some of it came back.

  • 1924The first fansAn event or moment

    The first documented fan clubs, organised around sheet music and radio rather than film. Following became joining.

  • 1940The bobby-soxersAn event or moment?

    Teenagers organised in public, in letterpress newsletters, to buy records and argue about a singer. A press of ten thousand.

1950-199912

mostly 4 events, 3 mechanisms and 2 studies. filed as application.
  • 1950Gazing at celebritiesA study or paper?

    The early frames on attraction to media figures, work that treated the person doing the gazing as a slightly odd specimen and the celebrity as an object of study rather than a participant.

  • 1950The fan clubA mechanism or tactic?

    A formal organisation with membership, dues, an elected president, a newsletter and rules. Not a spontaneous thing that happened to fans - a structure somebody built for them and that fans then ran themselves.

  • 1956Elvis PresleyA person?

    A national simultaneous viewing event, and an industry that learned within two years that the appeal was not the music.

  • 1959The manufactured teen idolA mechanism or tactic?

    A television family show repurposed as a launchpad: the first rock-and-roll teen idol was on screen from birth, and his parents edited his episodes into the earliest music videos. Assembled, yes - by television.

  • 1960The political parasocialA mechanism or tactic?

    Support for a politician structured as a personal bond rather than a policy preference, in a way that transfers poorly across candidates.

  • 1963Dionne Warwick's fan mailA person?

    She received correspondence by the sackful and answered it by name, individually, for years. The closest thing the twentieth century produced to a genuinely reciprocal parasocial relationship.

  • 1964BeatlesmaniaAn event or moment?

    A global mass hysteria, documented live, with screaming, fainting, and an industry that began pricing it within months.

  • 1968Charisma as a measurable assetA study or paper?

    Research on why some political candidates reliably produce personal attachment and others do not, despite comparable policy positions.

  • 1973Elvis, in a hologram, from HawaiiAn event or moment?

    Elvis Presley was filmed in a two-dimensional process that placed an apparently three-dimensional Elvis on stage, in a scene where real people were supposed to react to him. Aloha from Hawaii via Satellite reached an estimated half the planet.

  • 1993The Power Rangers handoverAn event or moment

    A costume is put on by a new actor and the audience transfers its entire attachment to whoever is now inside it. Running five days a week, in front of millions of children, with nobody pretending otherwise.

  • 1994Kurt CobainAn event or moment?

    A death broadcast in real time to a generation that had grown up with a songwriter's complaints as a soundtrack.

  • 1997The boy bandA work?

    Bands assembled for a market, with fan clubs, fan voting, fan merchandise and fan-financed releases.

2000-20096

mostly 3 events, 2 terms and people. filed as danger.
  • 2000Sasaeng cultureA term or concept?

    Organised, commercialised intrusion: tracking schedules, buying flight details, intercepting travel, surveilling buildings.

  • 2003The reality celebrityA person?

    A person famous for being filmed, with no skill, no work and no release schedule - and an audience that formed anyway.

  • 2005The character is not the personA term or concept?

    The pattern of locating and publishing a private address over a disliked fictional portrayal.

  • 2005The crowd injuryAn event or moment?

    Fans are injured or killed attempting to get closer to a performer, in volume, repeatedly, for years.

  • 2008The actor and the characterAn event or moment?

    An actor's persona and a character's persona are widely treated as the same thing, and one retires while the other continues.

  • 2009Michael JacksonAn event or moment?

    Died 25 June 2009, and within hours the crowd at his house was described by a firefighter as a circus, with tour buses at the gate. By August the coroner had called it a homicide and the autopsy was sealed.

2010+12

mostly 6 events, 3 mechanisms and 2 studies. filed as manipulation.
  • 2010Paid intimacyA mechanism or tactic?

    Paid message apps where a window shaped exactly like the one holding your best friend costs about four dollars a month per artist, plus anniversary counters that make the bond feel cumulative and streaks that convert it into a hostage situation.

  • 2010The lobbyAn event or moment?

    A fan harmed in an attempt to breach a celebrity's home, and a petition that followed.

  • 2012Fandom warsAn event or moment?

    Organised conflict between fanbases, with brigading, ratioing and coalition behaviour, conducted with the grammar of war.

  • 2015The failing endorsementA study or paper?

    Evidence that the value of a celebrity endorsement falls when the audience suspects the relationship is a transaction.

  • 2015The ratioA mechanism or tactic?

    A mass coordinated response in which the metric being weaponised is attention rather than truth.

  • 2016PrinceAn event or moment?

    A death announced through an initialism, and a global response before anybody knew the details.

  • 2016The K-pop intimacy economyA mechanism or tactic?

    Idol apps, paid messaging, fan-owned content, fan-voted releases, anniversary features, and a fandom documented and researched as a business model.

  • 2017CancellingA term or concept?

    Organised refusal to engage with a person, usually a public figure, over something they said or did.

  • 2019The hashtagAn event or moment?

    Fans of one artist are organised into mass harassment of another, using the first artist's parasocial bond as the weapon.

  • 2020Envy, relief, and the two feelings at onceA study or paper?

    Envy of the object of the attachment and simultaneous relief that it is not actually being withheld from you - the emotional ambivalence that is present in almost every long-term fan relationship and rarely discussed.

  • 2023The residencyAn event or moment?

    A single artist booking a stadium for many consecutive nights, generating a physical economy around one very large parasocial bond.

  • 2026A glimpse mistaken for accessAn event or moment?

    A season of a reality format and a global sporting event produce millions of viewers who then investigate the participants' personal lives. A relationship with an edited construct becomes a licence to intervene in a real life.
