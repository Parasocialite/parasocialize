The clerk · identity and rule

iYi

An eight lying on its side. Which is also infinity. Which is also two eyes. The lobes are the eyes - there is no head and no face, the whole glyph is doing the work. It is the clerk of the record, not the host of the site.

On ten section fronts, eight way pages, and one closing note. Never a masthead headline.

Where it appears

One mark, four jobs.

The rule is simple: iYi appears where it can mean something about the page, and nowhere it would only be decoration.

Section frontsTen pages, one expression each. The idol page winks; grief is sad; contest and danger are panic; brand is the clerk; the rest watch or go hollow. The expression is chosen from the section's subject, not rotated.
Way pagesEight pages, same rule. Persona winks; companion, divine and officer watch; character and cosmology go hollow; institution is the clerk; predator is panic.
The front pageOne closing note near the foot, with the clerk's sentence and a link back here. The masthead belongs to the wordmark and the two doors.
Entry pagesNo mascot. The title plate is that page's mark; adding a face would compete with the entry's own subject.

Eight expressions

One silhouette, so it reads as one character.

The two lobes are identical in every expression. That is the constraint that makes it a character and not a set of stickers - you know it is iYi at postage-stamp size because the shape never changes, only the face.

Use the drawing as drawn. The lobes are the eyes; do not add a face, a mouth or a body. Do not recolour the two sides into different hues - the dimmer right side is the second self, not a lighting effect. The mark should have clear space around it equal to the width of one lobe.

The rule

iYi never claims to remember you. The character is the clerk, and the clerk keeps a record; it does not know the reader. That is the line between a mascot and a false friend, and it is not negotiable.

No synthetic depictions of real people. The eyes are a drawing, not a likeness. The site does not generate a real person's face or voice to sell a bond, and the mascot is not used to soften that rule.

The eyes are the audience. iYi watches somebody who does not know iYi is there. The joke and the argument are the same thing, which is why the mark is allowed to be cute and is never allowed to be sentimental.

The standard the mascot is answerable to is the ledger. The record it keeps is the timeline.
